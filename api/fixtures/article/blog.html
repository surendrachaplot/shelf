Notes on sharpening a hand plane

Posted on 3 June 2026 by Tom

I put off learning to sharpen for about three years. I bought the plane, used it until it tore the wood instead of cutting it, and then left it on a shelf and felt bad about it.

What finally got me going was a neighbour who watched me fight a piece of oak for ten minutes and then, without saying anything, went home and came back with a stone.

The only three things that matter

The back of the blade has to be flat, at least for the last centimetre. If it is not, nothing you do to the bevel will help, because the edge is where two surfaces meet and you have only fixed one of them.

The bevel has to be one angle, not several. I use a cheap guide for this and I am not ashamed of it. Freehand is faster once you can do it, and for a long time you cannot.

And you have to take the burr off. That thin wire of steel you can feel with a fingernail is the proof you reached the edge; leave it on and it folds over on the first pass.

Most of what I read online skips straight to grit numbers, which is the least interesting part of the whole business and the easiest to get right.

A coarse stone to fix the shape, a fine one to finish, and a strip of leather if you are feeling fancy. That is the entire shopping list and it fits in a shoebox.

The first shaving I took afterwards came off in one piece, the full length of the board, thin enough to read through. I kept it. It is pinned above the bench.
