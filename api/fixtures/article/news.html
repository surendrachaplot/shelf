Harbour ferry returns after two years of repairs

By Mara Ellison
14 September 2026
Last updated on Monday at 9.40am.
4 min read

The Marlow Sound ferry carried passengers on Monday for the first time since the spring of 2024, ending a closure that outlasted two harbour masters and one council.

The Corran left the east quay at 7.10am with forty-one people aboard — most of them, by the skipper’s count, there for the crossing itself and not for anything on the far side.

It had been out of the water for 26 months. A routine survey found corrosion along the keel, and what was priced as a six-week job turned into a rebuild of most of the hull below the waterline.

What the engineers found

Surveyors from the yard at Port Kessock said the plating had thinned to less than half its original gauge in places. “You could have put a screwdriver through it,” said Iain Farrow, who led the work.

The council approved an extra £1.4m in March 2025. The final bill, published last week, was £3.1m, a little over twice the first estimate & the largest single item in the harbour budget.

For people on the north shore the difference is forty minutes each way. The road around the head of the sound is 31 miles; the crossing is nine minutes.

Ruth Adeyemi, who runs the bakery by the slipway, said she had kept the shop open through the closure mostly out of stubbornness. “Monday was the first morning in two years I sold out before ten.”

The timetable for the first month is hourly from 7am to 7pm. The board said a later sailing would be added once a second crew had been certified.
